A great man is not the one who never falls,
Nor the one who never hears failure's calls.
He is the one who rises when he's broken,
And keeps moving when hope feels forgotten.

He does not need the world to applaud,
He walks his path when the road feels hard.
While others sleep, he builds in silence,
Turning his weakness into defiance.

He doesn't chase comfort; he chases growth,
He keeps his promise when tested the most.
Discipline becomes the strength in his hand,
And patience teaches him how to stand.

One day they'll ask, "How did he become so strong?"
They won't see the nights he fought alone.
They'll only see the man standing tall—
Not knowing how many times he had to fall.

So don't wait for motivation.
Build discipline.
Don't wait for confidence.
Take action.
Don't fear failure.
Use it.

Because a great man isn't born great.

He becomes great—one difficult decision,
one disciplined day, and one more attempt at a time.
